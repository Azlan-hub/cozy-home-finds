When my wife and I first started decorating our apartment, we made absolutely every rookie mistake in the book. 

We thought that if we just bought all our furniture from the same matching collection, the apartment would automatically look like a professional catalog. Instead, it ended up looking like a cheap furniture showroom with zero personality. 

What we eventually realized is that a room looking "cheap" rarely has anything to do with how much money you spent on the couch. It almost always comes down to simple scaling, placement, and lighting errors.

If your space feels a little "off" but you can't figure out why, you might be making one of these five common decor mistakes. Here is exactly how we fixed them in our own home.

## 1. The "Matching Set" Trap

When we bought our first bedroom set, we bought the matching bed frame, the matching dresser, and the two matching nightstands. It felt safe. But safe is the enemy of expensive design. 

When every piece of furniture is made of the exact same wood tone or fabric, the room falls completely flat. 

**How to fix it:** Mix your materials. If you have a heavy wooden bed frame, break it up with painted, metal, or stone-topped nightstands. If your sofa is a cream linen, choose a warm oak or glass coffee table instead of a matching cream ottoman. Contrast is what makes a room look custom-built over time.

## 2. Floating Rug Syndrome

This is the number one mistake we see in small apartments. In an attempt to save money, people buy a tiny 4x6 rug and place it directly under the coffee table, so it looks like it's "floating" in the middle of the room.

Ironically, a rug that is too small actually makes the entire room shrink visually. 

**How to fix it:** Always size up. Your living room rug should be large enough that at least the front two legs of your sofa and any accent chairs sit comfortably on top of it. This "anchors" the furniture and immediately makes the floor plan feel expansive. 

## 3. The "Big Light" Dependency

If you have read any of our other posts, you know how strongly we feel about this. Relying entirely on the harsh overhead ceiling fixture casts terrible shadows, flattens the room's dimension, and makes your apartment feel like a waiting room.

**How to fix it:** Layered, warm ambient lighting. If you want a ceiling fixture, swap out the basic builder-grade dome for something with organic texture that diffuses the light beautifully.



## 4. Micro-Clutter on Surfaces

You can have a $5,000 sofa and a gorgeous marble coffee table, but if that table is covered in loose keys, crumpled receipts, four different remote controls, and hair ties, the room will instantly look chaotic and cheap. 

**How to fix it:** Corral the clutter. Every flat surface needs a designated "drop zone." Use aesthetic wooden or woven trays on your coffee table for the remotes. For larger items that pile up on the floor (like extra blankets and magazines), use mobile storage that actually looks like decor.



## 5. "High-Water" Curtains

We used to hang our curtains right directly on top of the window frame, and we bought panels that hovered two or three inches above the floor to keep them from getting dusty. This is the equivalent of wearing pants that are too short—it cuts off the visual line and makes your ceilings look incredibly low.

**How to fix it:** Use the "High and Wide" rule. Hang your curtain rod 4 to 6 inches *above* the window frame (as close to the ceiling as possible), and extend the rod a few inches past the sides of the window. Buy curtain panels that just barely "kiss" the floor. This optical illusion makes standard apartment windows look massive and luxurious.

## The Takeaway

Decorating a home is a process of trial and error. Don't beat yourself up if you have floating rugs or matching nightstands right now—we started there, too! By making these small, intentional tweaks to your layout, you can completely transform the energy of your space this weekend.
